# RL and learned search for MIP and scheduling: implications for ONTS

Reviewed 6 October 2026. This is a targeted primary-source review, not an exhaustive survey or a reproduced leaderboard. "State of the art" depends on the problem, solver, hardware, budget, initialization and metric. Published improvements on FJSP, routing or generic MIP do not establish improvements on ONTS.

The main recommendation is to improve **the search transitions and neighborhood support** before enlarging the policy. Our latest six-instance larger-case comparison gives fast PPO 0.197664, replay 0.195657 and the heuristic 0.197848. The positive supplementary PPO/replay interval comes from reused instances. Our graph/decoder ablations do not establish prefix or MILP-menu benefits. Those results support investigating search mechanisms, not declaring a superior RL architecture.

## MIP: distinguish the learned decisions

| [Gasse et al., NeurIPS 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01629), *Exact Combinatorial Optimization with Graph Convolutional Neural Networks* | Variable–constraint bipartite GNN imitates strong branching. **Imitation, not RL.** | A true coefficient graph differs from our compressed task–time graph. Useful structural baseline; requires solver-level branching access for direct reproduction. |
| [Nair et al., Neural Diving / Neural Branching manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13349) | Predict multiple partial integer assignments, complete sub-MIPs with SCIP; branching imitates an expert. | Learned initialization deserves a separate comparison. Changing the initial schedule must not be conflated with improving the repair controller. |
| [Sonnerat et al., Neural LNS manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.10201) | Solver-based expert teaches neighborhood selection after Neural Diving. **Imitation**, despite an MDP formulation. | Strengthen expert neighborhood labels before assuming RL must beat them. [Author code](https://github.com/google-deepmind/neural_lns). Publication venue not resolved in this review. |
| [Wu et al., NeurIPS 2021 RL-LNS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/fc9e62695def29ccdb9eb3fed5b4c8c8-Abstract.html) | Actor–critic learns parallel binary destruction decisions; IP solver repairs them. | Closest RL counterpart to factorized subsets. A task-level action is a structured compression of its variable-level selection. |
| [Huang et al., ICML 2023 CL-LNS](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/huang23g.html) | Variable–constraint attention; contrastive positive/negative neighborhoods from a slow local-branching expert. **Not RL.** | Strong teacher and negative examples are necessary controls. Gain-only imitation is not a CL-LNS reproduction. |
| [Yuan et al., ICLR 2025 BTBS-LNS](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/7eca17ef54789b0663cab421f2e9dbf5-Paper-Conference.pdf) | Variable–constraint–objective graph; learned destruction plus corrective release of fixed variables. | Test whether coordinated tasks are incorrectly excluded from repairs. ONTS binary activity does not need its generic-integer binarization. |
| [Scavuzzo et al., NeurIPS 2022 Tree MDPs](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/756d74cd58592849c904421e3b2ec7a4-Abstract-Conference.html) | Tree policy gradient assigns branching credit to descendant subtrees. | Our linear repair trajectory is not a B&B tree. Tree-specific credit assignment cannot be copied into PPO unchanged. |
| [Feng and Yang, AAAI 2025 SORREL](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/33219) | Offline RL from suboptimal branching demonstrations; online RL with selective self-imitation. | Test value-filtered experience rather than replaying every demonstration equally. The branching reward and action interface still differ. |
| [Tang et al., ICML 2020 Learning to Cut](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/tang20a.html) | RL selects cutting planes. | Solver-internal acceleration, requiring valid cut generation and callbacks, rather than task-group selection. |

| [Manoj et al., ICLR 2026 MIRACLE](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/0f8be7310d7ed5fca40f3b133d2394e9-Abstract-Conference.html) | PPO/GAIL cut-selection learning, curriculum and adaptive cut budgets. | Relevant resource-allocation ideas; cut callbacks are outside our present interface. Its source-reported gains concern particular SCIP settings, memory limits and success criteria, not a universal solver ranking. |
| [Strang et al., PlanB&B, April 2026 manuscript revision](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09219v4) | Model-based branching RL uses learned dynamics and planning. | Supports investigating lookahead, but optimizing B&B tree efficiency is distinct from short-budget ONTS primal improvement. Conference acceptance was not verified; retain manuscript status. |

| [EAS, ICLR 2022 manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05126) | Instance-specific adaptation of a small parameter subset. | Test only when the budget can pay for optimization; include all updates and reset between instances. |

## Screening and evidence limits

Newer is not automatically stronger. [HGT-Scheduler, March 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06777) is a useful relation-type ablation but its reported FT06 gap and limited FT10 study do not establish broad scheduling leadership. [Constraint Matters, accepted ICLR 2026 per author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18742) learns constraint/variable reduction, not RL; imposing predicted tight constraints restricts the feasible set and can exclude a better optimum. It motivates constraint information, not deleting battery constraints. [Neurosymbolic LNS, CP 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CP.2026.18) combines masked language models and CP on text/molecule tasks; it is outside this scheduling/RL comparison. [ReviBranch, 2025 manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17452) is a branching-history/reward-redistribution follow-up; venue and reproduction remain unchecked.

MIRACLE's full paper compares particular default/aggressive SCIP configurations under finite time and memory. Its limited learned-cut comparison does not constitute a broad solver-quality tournament; appendix results also vary across instance sets. We therefore do not import headline speedups into paper2. Likewise best-of-many samples, memory retrieval, rollout search and test-time learning consume compute that a single sampled ONTS trajectory does not.

## Proposed next experiment, ordered by information gained

These are recommendations, not implemented or evaluated components.

1. **Establish neighborhood headroom.** On training/validation instances only, compare long-budget full-MIP incumbents/bounds, whole-task release, task–time-block release and coordinated energy-exchange groups. Measure whether a strong oracle can improve a stalled heuristic. Retain full task validity when changing only a time block. If the repair interface cannot produce useful transitions, a larger GNN cannot create them.
2. **Separate current schedule from best feasible output.** Add feasible perturbations that actually allow temporary sacrifices, then compare monotone search, fixed annealing, tuned ALNS and learned acceptance. Merely allowing worse acceptance is insufficient if the repair always retains the current incumbent and optimizes the same objective. Reward best-so-far objective increments, exposing current/best gap, stagnation, history and remaining time. Store every accepted current state for independent feasibility replay.
3. **Add short lookahead and compact memory.** Compare one trajectory, independent restarts and small-beam one-to-three-step rollout search. Use PPO, imitation and random proposals inside the same search wrapper; compare recent-action features versus retrieved history. A deployment wrapper changes the executed behavior: training must record its actual likelihood or be explicitly search/distillation-based. Do not reuse simple categorical PPO likelihoods for best-of-many selection.
4. **Then test constraint-aware graphs and stronger supervision.** Compare task–time encoding against typed task/window/start/duration/recurrence/power/battery relations or an exact coefficient graph with block pooling. Include static-only, dynamic slack/history, linear/MLP and graph controls; pay graph extraction/inference costs. Compare contrastive/local-branching teachers and selective self-imitation with the same offline compute. Curriculum and small test-time adapters are later ablations, not automatic improvements.

## Benchmark required for paper2

Freeze untouched evaluation sets after selecting the new mechanism on development data. Use substantially more than six test instances and at least five independent policy seeds. Include held-out solar regimes, recurrence/window tightness, larger J/T and realistic ONTS/SatGNN instances after matching constraint semantics. Report 2/10/30/60-second anytime objectives and gaps where certified bounds exist, paired per-instance differences, win rates, failures, inference/repair costs, training compute and uncertainty. Instance sampling and policy-seed uncertainty are distinct.

Compare full solver, first-paper heuristic, random LNS, tuned adaptive LNS/annealing, a Balans-style bandit, imitation-only and replay-only. Add literature-method reproductions only when their interfaces and semantics genuinely match. Run both equal wall-clock and equal solver-work comparisons. Fix final checkpoint selection, thread counts, initialization and all timing boundaries. Exploration over many methods needs multiplicity-aware confirmation; an exploratory positive interval on examined cases is not a SOTA claim.

The scientific contribution should be a tested reason why ONTS search improves—such as overcoming energy-coupled neighborhood barriers—not a list of fashionable neural components.

## Additional recent sources verified during review

- [Liu et al., *Mixed-Integer Linear Optimization via Learning-Based Two-Layer Large Neighborhood Search*, LION 2025 proceedings published January 2026](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-09156-7_15), [primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08206): CL-TLNS combines contrastive neighborhood learning with inner/outer LNS to reduce repair difficulty. This is a non-RL comparator for the repair bottleneck; long-budget large-MIP results do not establish gains at two seconds.
- [Zhang et al., *Deep Reinforcement Learning Guided Improvement Heuristic for Job Shop Scheduling*, ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/c75abb33341363ee874a71f81dc45a3a-Abstract-Conference.html): complete-schedule N5 moves, separate current/best schedules and incumbent-improvement reward. Its shared-neighborhood tabu comparison is a useful fairness template.
- [DynaSchedBench, ICML 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/cao26z.html): calibrated dynamic-scheduling evaluation, rather than a new RL optimizer. Adapt the principle of measuring instance stress; its specific FJSP stress index is not an ONTS hardness measure.
- DANIEL's author preprint corresponds to the published IEEE TNNLS article, volume 35(3), 3091–3102 (2024), DOI [10.1109/TNNLS.2023.3306421](https://doi.org/10.1109/TNNLS.2023.3306421). Distinguish 2023 preprint date from 2024 volume date.
